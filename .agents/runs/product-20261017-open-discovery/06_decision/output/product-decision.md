# Product decision: test a specific buying decision before redesigning the site

Run `product-20261017-open-discovery` · October 17, 2026 UTC · previous run `product-20261016-site-direction-v2` · **Proposed, ready for validation planning only**. Fresh public research; reused October 16 growth evidence; incremental paid API cost **$0**. No interviews, tests, outreach, prototype, deployment or product implementation occurred.

## Decision in brief

**Keep the site small while testing whether we can resolve one consequential AI-software buying question better than the resources buyers already use. Do not commission a broad redesign yet.** At a hypothetical eight editorial hours/week, the first candidate is a **suitability evidence dossier**: a short, sourced answer to one buyer's actual workflow, ledger and eligibility constraints. At two hours/week, choose minimal change and establish measurement and audience access first.

Proposed audience: the person making a software decision for a small accounting firm. This is a research segment, not a validated customer. Proposed value proposition:

> Decide whether an AI accounting tool fits your situation, with the evidence, limitations and unanswered questions needed for the next conversation.

The distinctive result would be an answer to a specific unresolved decision—not another general ranking. It must demonstrably improve on existing comparators, vendor documentation and demos. “Sourced” alone is not differentiation.

Confidence is **low in product demand and traffic potential; moderate that this is a bounded, falsifiable next research allocation**. Workflow-pilot guidance remains the strongest challenger. Prefer it instead if recent user problems are primarily execution problems, existing selection resources suffice, and a qualified practitioner can review the work. Choose neither if we cannot reach intended users, resolve a real blocker or maintain accurate evidence.

This modest preference is a sequencing judgment: factual suitability questions can be checked before making process recommendations. It is not proof of lower actual production cost, a larger market or superior search potential. The existing bookkeeping prototype does not validate this choice. New competitive research weakens the earlier preference for a generic implementation guide; it does not prove a directory is the answer.

## Conditional product definition and journey

Start with **one question from a recent real decision**, two or three relevant candidates, and explicit scope. Ledger compatibility is an example, not an inherited requirement. A question that a vendor FAQ already answers fully does not qualify.

Primary journey: a buyer finds a specific compatibility/fit answer → checks whether the audience, region and workflow match → sees sourced facts, contradictions and unknowns → records a candidate and the next question for its vendor → shares that decision note with a colleague. Return is episodic, when scope or evidence changes; no daily habit is assumed.

Public content model, only after validation: a question page with applicability and answer; a compact evidence table with claim/source/date/status; a copyable decision note containing candidate, constraint, uncertainty and next step; a method/corrections page. The answer remains readable without signup. Profiles exist only to support an actual question. No comprehensive directory, global score, chatbot, account system, newsletter promise, job board, course credential or workflow-execution app in initial scope.

## Operating model

Initial work estimates: **C1 8–14 editorial hours** for one researched question and test document; **C2 12–20 hours** plus practitioner scheduling; recruitment additional for both. These are planning assumptions, not measured production costs or evidence of efficiency.

At **2 hours/week**, keep scope minimal: measurement diagnosis, recruitment and one learning step at a time. No maintained catalog or recurring publication promise. At **8 hours/week**, hypothetical recurring allocation is 3 hours evidence/refresh,2 hours user learning/reviewer coordination,2 hours distribution,1 hours measurement/corrections. Initial preparation displaces publishing rather than being free extra work.

Name one accountable editor before public release. Qualified practitioner review is required for consequential accounting/process interpretation; factual source extraction is not a substitute. Check critical eligibility facts before release, on material source changes and at a proposed 30-day checkpoint; label overdue facts and suspend affected recommendations. Time the first production and two refreshes. If work does not fit agreed capacity with distribution and learning time remaining, narrow scope or stop. Paid product access, participant compensation and outreach budgets are unapproved and excluded. AI may assist research; it does not supply user evidence or expertise.
