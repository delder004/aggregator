# Product concepts made tangible

These are Markdown concept sketches, not built interfaces or validated user stories.

## A — The workflow change desk

Promise: “Know what changed in your workflow—and what deserves your next conversation.” First visit begins at a precise search answer; a repeat visit starts at a dated short briefing. Journey: recognize workflow → see decision-relevant delta → inspect evidence and applicability → take a team note → return when the record changes.

Illustrative editorial treatment using historical Xero sources from stage 02:

**Document capture: revisit the handoff, not the whole ledger.**

“Xero's July post treated tax extraction as forthcoming. Its September update describes it as available and moves the old Files entry to Documents. Before recycling your old instructions, confirm your account's eligibility and update the navigation reference. Neither post establishes that your reviewers can skip checking extracted data.”

Visible hierarchy: decision headline; affected workflow/account scope; a two-row July/September evidence history; our interpretation; one unanswered question; copyable team note; sources. This is an illustration based on publisher statements, not a current product test or accounting recommendation. The first action is a check, not an unearned “switch vendors.”

Team note: “We should revisit our document-intake instructions. Evidence changed between July and September. Verify availability in our account; assign an owner to check menu location and extraction behavior. Keep our existing review step until our own test supports a change.”

Taste: restrained, concrete, selective. A source must change a decision to lead. A funding announcement without a workflow consequence goes to the background archive. Show “no material change” instead of manufacturing urgency. No global AI score or implication that more automation is always better. Put one important uncertainty beside the conclusion, not in a footer. No feed of identically sized cards.

A generic version would summarize each announcement separately; the proposed product remembers what the earlier answer was and explains why it changed.

## B — The suitability desk

Promise: “Leave with a shortlist you can defend.” First visit: a buyer asks whether they can preserve their ledger. Repeat: a plan or constraint changes; revisit the saved memo (initially a copyable artifact, no account required).

Illustrative question, no vendor findings implied: **“Keep the ledger. Change the document intake. Which candidates still fit?”** Page order: constraints → include/exclude/unknown table → evidence per conclusion → three questions for a demo. Fictional examples use “Candidate A/B,” never real unsupported vendor facts. Example result: “A remains under consideration; B is unresolved because the integration claim does not identify the required plan. Ask for that plan before scheduling implementation.”

Taste: fewer candidates and explicit disqualification, no numeric winners. Unknown is a usable result; avoid false precision. Show the exact constraint behind every exclusion. Useful output: a five-line purchasing memo. Strong experience, but needs concrete user question and likely returns only when the purchase changes.

## C — The accountant practice lab

Promise: “Practice the judgment AI cannot supply for you.” First visit: a ten-minute synthetic exercise. Repeat: a harder case exposing a different error, not an endless newsfeed.

Illustrative exercise: **“The model's tidy summary missed a duplicate. Can you find it?”** Supply three synthetic source records, a generated summary and an explicit task: identify duplicated evidence, cite the conflicting lines, and explain what cannot yet be concluded. No real client data. Reveal a practitioner-reviewed reasoning rubric after submission; do not equate agreement with a model to correctness.

Taste: calm workbook, one task per page, evidence before answer, feedback explaining reasoning. No gamified accuracy score hiding ambiguous cases, no employment promises. Useful output: a worked review note the learner can compare with a qualified explanation. Memorable experience and progression, but original case production and trustworthy feedback require an educator/practitioner capability we have not secured.

## Preference

A has the best potential to turn this site's collection habit into something readers use repeatedly. B is clearer for search but less distinctive without actual buying tasks. C is appealing but changes the enterprise into a teaching product. The useful part of A is a maintained decision record; a weekly email alone is not this product.
