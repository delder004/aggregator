# Ambition: make accounting AI change intelligible enough to act on

October 17, 2026 UTC · v3 · Proposed.

The user's goal is sustained relevant traffic, supported by a maintainable local growth/research process. They explicitly want a product process with vision, taste and strategic judgment, with a Markdown proposal as the final output. They have not selected an audience, publication cadence, business model or staffing budget. Approval to reshape and research is not approval to build.

The existing design roadmap proposes a focused data product with editorial judgment. Develop its strongest idea—an argument before a feed—but challenge its proxy metrics: coverage volume and job counts do not themselves tell a practitioner what changed. A visually polished ledger of mentions could still be a feed in disguise.

My proposed belief: as AI becomes an ordinary feature inside accounting products, the useful question shifts from “which tools have AI?” to “which part of my work can change, for whom, and what still needs judgment?” This is a strategic hypothesis, not a finding from interviews. The long-term ambition is a dependable record of those changes, useful both for finding an answer today and revisiting a decision later.

Do not lock in the firm leader yet. Compare the person deciding how their firm works, the software buyer deciding what to purchase, and the accountant deciding what to learn. Their outcomes differ enough to imply different products.

Assets: collection infrastructure and historical topic coverage, an existing indexed domain, a local evidence CLI, and an editorial design direction. These are conveniences, not a moat. We must earn practitioner trust, a consistent interpretation method, usable history and distribution. No requirement to preserve existing infrastructure beyond maintainability.

Refuse to optimize for the number of vendors listed, articles ingested, generic AI headlines or speculative job-loss anxiety. Favor a smaller number of pages that remain useful after publication. This sacrifices breadth and some incidental search traffic.

Research questions: Are the relevant answers already easy to obtain? Can a living record expose meaningful deltas between source announcements? Is the urgent job selection, implementation, monitoring or learning? Can the first release deliver recurring usefulness without claiming unearned practitioner authority? What would make a reader forward a page to a colleague?
