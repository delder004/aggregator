# Product proposal: the accounting workflow change desk

October 17, 2026 UTC · pipeline v3 · run `product-20261017-vision-led` · **Proposed**. Previous run: `product-20261017-open-discovery`. Research and product proposal only; no implementation, publishing, outreach or experiments executed. Incremental paid API cost: $0.

## The direction

**Make agenticaiccounting.com the working reference a small accounting firm's operations lead uses to decide what AI changes deserve attention in the way their team works.** Start with document intake and review, then earn the right to cover adjacent bookkeeping workflows.

The promise: **understand what changed, whether it applies, and what to revisit with your team.** A visitor should leave with a better next decision, not a longer reading list.

My strategic belief is that the enduring editorial opportunity is in the changing boundary between automated work and human review. The interesting unit is a workflow: what goes in, what the software does, what a person still checks, and which new evidence changes the answer. That belief is proposed judgment, not an interview finding. The site should become a maintained reference with a point of view; a dated briefing brings readers back to it.

Why now: product capabilities and their descriptions evolve across announcements. This run found a concrete historical example in Xero's July and September document-capture posts [S1, S2]. Why us: we have a collection system, an indexed domain and a local evidence pipeline. Those reduce setup friction. They do not confer authority. The advantage to earn is a carefully maintained history of consequential workflow changes, joined to clear editorial decisions and eventually practitioner-reviewed firsthand observations.

We choose continuity over breadth. We will miss general AI news, investor attention and some high-volume queries. We will not rank every product or turn every launch into an alert. The strongest alternative is a buyer suitability desk. It offers clearer purchase intent, but it is mostly episodic; I prefer a recurring workflow decision as the basis for an audience that returns outside software-buying cycles.

## The experience we want to create

Imagine a firm operations lead preparing the next team process meeting. They arrive through a precise search question, find a maintained workflow page, and can answer: “Does this change something we currently do?” On a later visit they see only material changes since the previous edition, including reasons to leave a process alone. Their useful output is a short note for a colleague, with evidence and a specific unresolved question.

The homepage begins with a dated editorial judgment and its scope. Then it shows the affected workflow and a small number of consequential changes. The underlying evidence and background wire follow. The primary action is **Read the workflow decision**, followed by **Take this to your team**. There is no signup wall before the answer.

### A worked editorial specimen

The following is an **illustrative product treatment of historical publisher statements**, not a current eligibility check, firsthand product evaluation, or operational accounting instruction.

> **Document intake: re-evaluate the capture step before changing the review step.**
>
> The opportunity is to examine whether a capture capability already in your ledger can reduce a separate handoff. The evidence does not justify removing human review or replacing an existing capture tool without a scoped comparison.

The supporting record would distinguish three things:

| Layer | What the specimen says | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Dated source facts | Xero's July 2 post places tax extraction in its future list. The September 18 post describes it among available capabilities and retains human review. [S1, S2] | The earlier feature status needs revision. These dates do not establish availability for every plan or region today. |
| Editorial interpretation | A firm already using that ledger may have a reason to revisit its document-intake handoff before shopping for another tool. | Investigate an existing capability first; this is a prioritization judgment, not a recommendation to consolidate tools. |
| Unanswered decision | Does the firm's own account handle its relevant document types and exceptions well enough to change the current handoff? | Keep the current process until the firm checks applicability and compares its actual needs. Source extraction alone cannot answer this. |

**Copyable team note, illustrative:**

> Revisit document intake at our next process review. The published capability description changed; our old comparison may now be stale. Ask the process owner to check account eligibility and compare a small, approved sample against the existing handoff. Keep the current review requirement. Report exception types and review effort, not just whether extraction worked. If it adds checking or misses the cases we care about, retain the current setup.

The editorial value is the bounded decision and the connection to the earlier record. A navigation rename is useful maintenance, but it is not the product's reason to exist. We also cannot claim that this specimen beats the latest vendor article; that comparison is the first test.

On the next edition, the page might report a material change, a corrected interpretation, or **no change to our conclusion**. It must not manufacture novelty to satisfy a publishing quota. The reader returns to revisit an active workflow decision, not because we promised a daily dose of AI.

### Taste with observable consequences

- Lead with the decision and affected workflow. A company announcement leads only when it changes that decision.
- Put the essential uncertainty beside the conclusion. Dates and source status remain visible; no badges suggesting independent testing when none occurred.
- Use compact comparison rows for evidence and short prose for interpretation. On narrow screens, preserve the conclusion, scope and uncertainty before secondary detail.
- Sound like a thoughtful colleague: “revisit this handoff” is more useful than “AI is revolutionizing accounting.” Avoid alarm about job displacement and unexplained maturity scores.
- Make restraint visible. A “no material change” entry and an explicit reason to keep a process are legitimate editorial outcomes.
- Give one workflow room to breathe. No equal-weight card grid mixing jobs, funding, research papers and vendor releases. The existing wire can remain a secondary archive.

These choices develop the existing design roadmap's editorial data-product ambition. They reject its temptation to use article counts or hiring counts as a substitute for an argument. This proposal does not change the roadmap or scheduled agents.

## Why this direction

The user asked for traffic growth, a maintainable local research process, and a product direction with taste. They did not choose this audience, set a staffing budget, or endorse the recommendation. The operations-lead situation is an inferred target. No participant interviews occurred.

We compared three serious bets and minimal change:

| Dimension | Workflow change desk — preferred | Buyer suitability desk | Accountant practice lab | Minimal change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Useful result | A justified process-review question | A defensible shortlist | A better worked review of AI output | Existing information access |
| Experience and appeal | Briefing connected to a living answer | Constraints, exclusions and a copyable buying memo | Synthetic case, attempted answer, expert feedback | Familiar feed |
| Distinction to earn | Cross-time workflow interpretation | Specific constraints incumbents leave unresolved | Authentic exercises and good feedback | None established |
| Discovery | Workflow and feature questions | Compatibility and comparison queries | Learning and task exercises | Existing indexed pages |
| Return/share | Changes to an active workflow; team brief | Next purchase or changed shortlist | Progression and peer practice | Unproven feed habit |
| Accumulating asset | Claim history and revised decision rationale | Fit records and buyer questions | Cases, rubrics and consented error patterns | More coverage, uncertain value |
| Main cost/risk | Continuous editorial accuracy and authority | Maintenance with episodic demand | Practitioner teaching and assessment | Continued weak traffic |
| Current evidence | Historical source delta; no user validation | Broad-topic interest; no dossier demand | Official training supply; no learner demand | Sparse actual search traffic |

The lab is attractive: “find the duplicate the AI summary missed” makes learning tangible, with a synthetic record set and a qualified reasoning rubric. But it requires a teaching capability we have not secured and takes the site into a different enterprise. The buyer desk is the strongest challenger: a “keep the ledger, change intake” comparison can resolve a buying blocker. The workflow desk retains that decision depth while giving users a reason to revisit existing work without buying anything.

Public inspection found substantial substitutes. Atoz combines directory, editorial views and personalized monitoring/comparison [S3]. CPA.com offers decision resources, a newsletter and practical use cases [S4, S5]. Rightworks offers guided firm development [S6]. Microsoft provides sequenced business AI learning [S7]. Accounting Today's technology category provides a broad, authored news stream [S8]. Their presence does not eliminate an opportunity, but it makes “curated AI news” an inadequate distinction. Public positioning is not evidence that their products fail—or succeed—for our intended reader.

## Vision, first release and first experiment

**Vision:** the dependable record of how accounting workflows change, connecting what software can do to what people should reconsider. Over time it could include practitioner-reviewed firsthand evaluations and histories across intake, reconciliation and close. No autonomous accounting execution is envisioned.

**Coherent first release:** one complete document-intake desk, with one maintained workflow page, two dated briefing editions, and evidence histories for two or three relevant products. Select those products through actual source coverage and user relevance; do not pad to three. At least two completed, relevant product records and a substantive comparison are release acceptance criteria, not research already completed here. Include a usable team note, RSS, and a correction/history page. Each edition must contain a finished answer and a consequential interpretation—or explicitly no change. An empty taxonomy, directory shell or collection of placeholders does not deliver the promise.

Its essential loop is **entry question → current workflow answer → evidence change → team note → next edition**. No account or personalization is needed. A single-vendor source pair can demonstrate the format in the experiment, but a public desk should reconcile enough relevant alternatives to avoid being a repackaged vendor blog. If the research cannot produce that, it is not ready for release.

**First experiment:** a complete Markdown desk specimen and two editions evaluated with five actual firm operations leads, against their current information sources. This is smaller than the release and tests the central recurring job. It is proposed, not executed. No participants or reviewer are secured.

Non-goals: a comprehensive vendor directory, global winner score, jobs board expansion, course platform, chatbot, account system, daily newsletter, client-data integration or broad visual overhaul before the product contract is agreed. Keep useful existing indexed destinations until a later migration plan maps real replacements.

## How it earns an audience

The growth model is **specific question discovery → maintained useful answer → internal sharing and later revisit → a better historical reference → more specific answers and citations**. The compounding asset is not scraped article volume. It is the editorial record of a claim, its scope, what superseded it, and why that changed a process decision. Public facts are copyable; the history and trust take work. Neither is a moat on day one.

Choose two acquisition routes first: search and reader-to-colleague sharing. Search reaches a person with an immediate question. The team note makes the answer useful in a group decision. RSS supports return; it is not itself an acquisition engine. Avoid a broad social publishing schedule until a format proves useful.

| Intent/channel | Proposed artifact | Access and measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Feature availability and workflow change: e.g. “Xero document capture tax extraction,” “AI document capture review process” | Exact-scope current answer linked to history | Owned domain, but exact volumes/ranking unknown. Measure fixed page/query GSC cohorts; examples are research seeds, not proven keywords. |
| Comparing handoffs: e.g. “native document capture versus separate intake tool,” “document capture exception review” | Decision-oriented workflow comparison | Publishable if researched; do not mass-generate pages. Demand unmeasured. Track qualified task success separately from search clicks. |
| Internal forwarding | Self-contained team note with stable source URL and reviewed date | Depends on readers finding it useful. Test voluntary forwarding/reuse with consent, not a share-button count. |
| Practitioner/newsletter citation | A narrow, original evidence-history finding | No relationship or placement secured. Pursue only after useful material exists and outreach is separately authorized. |
| RSS/bookmark revisit | Next dated edition plus changed workflow record | Owned surface; existing audience size unknown. RSS requests are not unique readers. Use consenting follow-up for actual return usefulness. |

Measure acquisition with finalized GSC clicks/impressions by stable scope. Measure task usefulness in observed sessions: can readers identify applicability, explain a meaningful change and produce an accurate next-step note? Measure repeat value through consenting follow-up across editions. The current cookieless setup cannot support durable-user retention claims.

For every eventual release, record timestamp, commit, affected URLs, hypothesis, primary metric and concurrent changes with the local growth CLI's `change add`. Recollect equal finalized 28-day windows with identical scope and use `impact`; its sparse-click warning is a heuristic, not statistical proof. New pages have no same-page pre-release baseline, so report discovery descriptively. Record content revisions and distribution separately. This research run is not a deployment and creates no fake change event.

## Operating model and sequence

Actual capacity and expertise are unknown. Proposed initial editorial preparation is **20–35 hours** for the bounded release, plus practitioner review and recruitment; implementation and any paid product access are separate. These are planning estimates, not observed costs.

One accountable editor must own interpretation and corrections. AI can find and compare source statements; it cannot invent a user need or confer accounting expertise. A qualified practitioner reviews consequential process implications before public release. No reviewer is currently secured. If that capability is unavailable, do not quietly replace review with an AI confidence label.

Check critical claims at publication, after a material source update, and at a proposed 30-day review deadline. Reduce coverage when review is overdue. Record source date, retrieval date, scope and status; distinguish announcements, documented availability and firsthand observations. A source changing silently is a reason to recheck, not automatic evidence that all customers received a feature.

At a hypothetical **two hours/week**, retain the vision but cover one workflow on a monthly cadence: roughly one hour for checking/interpretation and one for reader learning, distribution and measurement, averaged across weeks. This may prove insufficient; time the work and pause publication if review quality cannot fit. At **eight hours/week**, aim for one fortnightly edition, averaging three hours research/maintenance, two editorial/reviewer coordination, two user learning/distribution and one measurement/corrections weekly. Initial work displaces the cadence. Neither scenario is a commitment supplied by the user.

Sequence: agree the direction → secure editorial/reviewer capability and recruit a small test cohort → complete and compare the two-edition specimen → measure production cost → separately authorize the bounded release and instrumentation → expand only after recurring usefulness. A later redesign should express this product hierarchy, not precede it. Reconcile the stylist roadmap and automated-agent instructions before any implementation; preserve or carefully redirect existing indexed URLs.

## What could make this wrong

The strongest objection: this is an expensive newsletter with a table, and readers can get the same answer from the newest vendor post. The decisive question is whether remembering and interpreting change makes an actual recurring decision better.

The following thresholds are practical decision rules for small tests, not statistical validation:

| Rank / assumption | Proposed smallest test and effort | Required access | Continue / revise / stop and strategic consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The recurring job exists and we add value | Five operations leads bring a recent process-review question. Compare a complete Markdown specimen with their usual sources; alternate order. Estimate 8–14 hours preparation plus 4–6 hours sessions/synthesis, excluding recruitment. | Five suitable participants and a qualified reviewer; neither secured. | Continue if at least 4/5 form an applicable, accurate note and 3/5 identify a decision-relevant insight beyond their usual sources. Revise once if only comprehension fails. If ordinary sources suffice, stop the desk direction or choose the buyer desk if actual tasks are predominantly purchase decisions. |
| 2. Value recurs outside buying | Show a second meaningful edition after 2–4 weeks; ask participants to describe any use in an actual meeting/task. Estimate 4–8 hours editorial/follow-up. | Consenting follow-up and a material source change; no fabricated novelty. | Continue if at least 3/5 can show a concrete second use or requested update tied to work. Praise or stated subscription intent alone fails. If value is one-off, revise toward a maintained reference or buyer desk, not a recurring briefing claim. |
| 3. Interpretation is trustworthy and maintainable | Time one full record and two refreshes; reviewer checks all consequential interpretations and critical source claims. | Named editor/reviewer, agreed real capacity. | Continue only with no unresolved critical error and recurring effort fitting capacity while leaving distribution time. Shrink coverage/cadence once; if still infeasible, stop the publication model. Do not trade accuracy for frequency. |
| 4. Intended readers can be reached | After useful task results, one bounded public release and separately authorized targeted distribution effort. Four-week acquisition effort, with search evaluated over longer finalized windows. | Working analytics; permission and actual access for any outreach. | Continue expansion when intended readers arrive and complete/use the task; incidental clicks do not count. If exposure is too small, result is inconclusive, not rejection. If no accessible route emerges within agreed effort, pause expansion and change distribution before adding content. |

No revenue model is assumed. A later paid research or sponsorship model must preserve editorial independence and would require a separate decision. The immediate objective is useful traffic and an audience with a reason to return.

## What improved from the previous run

The previous final recommended testing a buying dossier and defaulting to minimal change at low capacity. That was a cautious allocation decision, not a compelling destination. This run selects a product with a recurring job, makes its character tangible, and separates the vision, release and test. Its first release preserves the essential loop instead of presenting one research document as the whole product.

The change of winner is primarily **new strategic judgment**, not new market validation. Fresh inspection contributed the historical Xero feature-status change and a more concrete competitive experience comparison. The baseline analytics remain the same. The design roadmap is explicitly developed and challenged. The old run's evidence discipline survives; its reluctance to propose a direction does not.

Remaining weaknesses: no user interviews, no verified channel access, no secured practitioner reviewer, no measured production cost, and uneven quantitative demand research. The proposal is worth testing because the experience is coherent, not because these weaknesses disappeared.
