# Product proposal: the accounting workflow compatibility reference

October 17, 2026 UTC · Pipeline v3.1 · Run `product-20261017-evidence-led` · Prior run: `product-20261017-vision-led` · **Proposed; not approved for implementation.**

## The direction

Make agenticaiccounting.com the reference an accounting-firm operations lead opens when a new AI feature might change an existing software workflow.

**The promise: understand what can change without losing sight of the handoff.** Start with a concrete question—can native capture replace this part of our intake process?—and show what goes in, what each tool does, where review happens, and what comes out. Preserve the evidence and the history behind the answer.

Our proposed strategic belief is that overlapping AI features make workflow boundaries more useful than product labels. “Both capture invoices” says little about whether two routes fit the same process. A well-made reference can connect documentation that is currently scattered across vendors. It should feel like a clear, independent working note that deserves a place in a team discussion.

This develops the existing roadmap's **focused data product with editorial judgment**. It narrows the previous run's workflow change desk: the maintained answer becomes the product; change briefings serve that answer. A news habit is no longer assumed. We give up comprehensive coverage, global rankings, a jobs-led homepage and a daily publishing obligation.

Why now: Xero's dated announcements show native capture expanding, while Dext documents alternative publishing and approval routes [S1–S4]. Why us is still an advantage to earn: consistent scope, careful synthesis and a useful historical record. Existing aggregation can find leads; it does not supply expertise or trust. Public facts are copyable, and a general AI assistant could reproduce much of the synthesis. Our work must stay useful enough to revisit.

## The experience we want to create

### A worked specimen: “Can native Xero capture replace our intake handoff?”

**Illustrative page and reader situation, using real published source claims.** A firm already using Xero is reconsidering the intake of a supported unpaid supplier Cost invoice. The external-approval example is specifically Dext’s Invoice destination without a bank-linked payment method allocation. This is a software-boundary comparison, not accounting advice or a recommendation to remove a control. No product was tested. Region, account, plan and configuration must be specified before an actual adoption decision; the records below are scoped public descriptions, not universal availability claims.

The page leads with an editorial conclusion:

> Capture overlap does not establish an equivalent handoff. Compare the point at which the record reaches Xero and the separate approval route—not just whether a tool extracts an invoice.

| Workflow dimension | Native Xero capture | Dext directly to Xero | Dext through ApprovalMax to Xero |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope/input | Receipts and bills enter Xero Documents by upload, email or photo, according to its September post. | Dext capture precedes publication to an existing Xero organisation. | Adds an external approval route to Dext's processed documents. |
| Documented action | Capture/extract and match or create a transaction. | Publishing sends item data and its source attachment to the selected destination. | For the scoped Invoice route, Dext sends the document for configured approval; ApprovalMax syncs it onward after approval. |
| Review boundary | Xero's post preserves reader review/approval. | Review and publishing permission are separate from the status selected for publication. A status alone is not evidence of an external authorization process. | The documented route includes a configured approval workflow before the Xero handoff. |
| Output relevant to this question | The September post places direct bill creation into awaiting-approval/payment states in future plans. | Purchases publishing offers status choices, subject to plan restrictions. | Documented Xero output after approval is Awaiting Payment, with an attached audit trail. |
| Eligibility to resolve | September global announcement does not establish availability for every account or region. | Active connection and publishing permission; Simple restricts bill publication, and Cashbook/Ledger differ. | Dext admin access, active ledger connection in ApprovalMax and an activated Bill Review and Approval workflow. |
| Evidence status | Vendor announcement, September 18, 2026 [S1]. | Vendor help and overview, opened October 17; exact update date unavailable [S3, S5, S6]. | Vendor integration help, opened October 17; exact update date unavailable [S4]. |

**Do not generalize the external-approval arrow:** Dext documents Bank as bypassing ApprovalMax. For its Xero Invoice destination, a bank-linked payment method allocation also sends the item directly to Xero as paid; the Paid toggle does not determine that behavior. Credit notes/refunds and Sales items are excluded from this integration route [S4]. This is a documented routing boundary, not an instruction to change payment or approval settings. Likewise, Xero’s announcement about a future direct-creation state does not establish that Xero lacks approval workflows generally.

These are **alternative route descriptions**, not three equivalent product packages or an exhaustive stack comparison. Native extraction is not being compared with external approval as if they were the same feature. Prices, extraction quality, security configuration and complete plan eligibility are outside this specimen; it cannot establish overall replacement fitness.

The useful result is a narrower software question: *Which output and approval boundary does our actual process require, and which documented route describes it?* The reader can distinguish “a field was extracted,” “a record was published with a status,” and “a configured approval route ran.” That distinction is the editorial contribution. The feature claims remain the vendors'.

A copyable team note follows:

> **Question:** Can the intake step change while preserving the required downstream handoff?  
> **What this reference establishes:** the documented routes differ at publication and approval; routing exceptions mean an integration label does not prove every item enters approval.  
> **Still to resolve:** our market, plan, configured route, intended output and account-level behavior.  
> **Owner/checkpoint:** [team fills in].  
> **Evidence:** source links and this reference's review date.  
> **Decision:** open; no migration or control change recommended by this note.

No signup interrupts this result. A return visit shows changes to the same answer, not a fresh carousel of loosely related headlines.

### What changed, and what did not

A small reusable content record contains: **workflow, route, market/plan scope, input, action, review point, output, source URL/date, retrieval date, evidence status, uncertainty, superseded claim and editorial implication**. A route can have several claims; one marketing page cannot set every field to “verified.”

Historical example: Xero's July 2 US-site post listed tax extraction as forthcoming; its September 18 global post described it among new capabilities [S1, S2]. Update the record from “future in July publication” to “described as new in September publication,” retaining both contexts. Do not infer the exact rollout date or coverage of every account. The interpretation changes the capture comparison, but it does not establish an equivalent publishing/approval handoff. That is a meaningful update without inventing a product test or a new edition published by us.

### Taste with consequences

Lead with the question and a clear scoped judgment. Place the route table next, evidence history after that, and a usable note last. Use restrained typography and compact rows; dates and uncertainties belong beside claims. Prefer one complete answer to ten thin pages. Refuse universal winner badges, unsupported precision, generic AI enthusiasm, and article counts as proof of value. A useful “no change to this boundary” is better than a forced announcement. This is a product/editorial specification, not a completed visual design.

## Why this direction

Three audiences were explored: firm operations leads revisiting a process, buyers selecting or renewing tools, and staff learning AI-assisted work. These are inferred situations, not interviewed users.

| Direction | Appeal and growth mechanism | Advantage to earn / principal weakness | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workflow compatibility reference | Answers a concrete process question; specific search entry, team sharing and later reference use. | Comparable records and reasons for revisions; documentation may already suffice. | Preferred: our worked evidence supports a narrow, legible contribution. |
| Buying desk | Clear shortlist at a high-intent decision; comparisons and purchase memos. | Tested fit knowledge; strong existing segmented answers. | Strongest challenger. Prefer it if actual tasks are predominantly buying. |
| AI practice lab | Synthetic cases, reasoning and feedback; progression and educator recommendation. | Reviewed teaching corpus; expertise and access not secured. | Attractive different business, insufficient basis to prioritize now. |
| Minimal change | Preserve existing indexed pages and improve measurement. | Low commitment, no new audience promise. | Allocation fallback while learning is blocked, not the vision. |

A lab specimen would ask a learner to spot a synthetic mismatch between an invoice total and an AI summary, explain evidence, then inspect a reviewed answer. CPA.com already provides concrete use cases, and Microsoft offers structured learning [S9, S10]. The distinction would have to be practice and feedback, not prompts. The buying concept would lead with disqualifying requirements and an eligible shortlist; it currently lacks a superior answer to the inspected comparator.

Competition argues for focus, not an empty niche. Atoz already describes structured workflow information and editorial judgment, and discloses a Sift/AccountingWEB media relationship [S8]. We have no equivalent access. “Independent, sourced and opinionated” alone is insufficient positioning.

## Vision, first release and first experiment

**Vision:** a trusted record of how software moves accounting work, eventually including practitioner-tested configurations and histories across intake, reconciliation and close. It becomes useful because a reader can connect a new claim to an existing process, not because it tracks the most vendors.

**Coherent first release:** one complete intake-to-Xero reference with native and external routes, a meaningful comparison, dated claim history, copyable team note, correction/staleness handling and a selective change feed. Select and verify one target market and relevant plan scope with intended readers before public release. UK-facing search results and venue research suggest a research entry point; they do not justify assuming all readers are UK-based or applying global product claims uniformly. Draft sources inspected here are not a finished production inventory.

**First experiment:** evaluate this complete Markdown specimen against the best substitutes with five relevant operations leads and a qualified reviewer. It tests the central benefit without a website. No participants or reviewer are secured, and the experiment has not run.

Non-goals: a comprehensive directory, daily newsletter, pricing leaderboard, chatbot, accounts, client-data ingestion, certification or broad visual overhaul. Preserve existing indexed destinations until an implementation proposal maps replacements. The current stylist roadmap is not silently superseded by this research.

## How it earns an audience

**Specific question → useful boundary comparison → team note or citation → later reference/revision → a stronger historical record.** Search and internal sharing are the first acquisition bets; RSS serves return rather than creating an audience by itself. The accumulating asset is compatible claim history and earned trust, not scraped volume.

A concrete entry opportunity is **“Xero smart document capture awaiting approval Dext.”** Generic web search on October 17 surfaced vendor help, Xero's update and a product-ideas discussion. A page explaining capture versus publication/approval boundaries could offer a useful synthesis. The broader “Xero smart document capture vs Dext” already surfaced a substantive comparison; do not compete merely by making it longer. Searches were location/device-unspecified answer discovery, not controlled ranking measurements or evidence of traffic volume. Exact demand remains unknown.

One researched distribution venue is AccountingWEB. Its public blogging rules require relevant, substantive content, discourage self-promotion and bare external links, and limit frequency [S11]. A possible contribution is a self-contained explanation of why an approval-state label and an approval route deserve separate columns. This would need a suitable author and actual posting eligibility; neither placement nor audience access is established. No outreach or posting occurred. Do not treat Atoz's media partnership as a route we can copy on demand.

Measure acquisition with finalized GSC page/query cohorts. Measure usefulness through observed tasks: can a reader explain the relevant boundary and uncertainty accurately, and make an appropriate team note? Measure return value through consenting follow-up on a real later question or material update, not anonymous cookies across days. Sharing-button clicks and RSS requests are intermediate signals, not readers or outcomes.

After any separately authorized release, record timestamp, commit, URLs, hypothesis, metric and concurrent changes with the local growth CLI's `change add`. Recollect equal finalized 28-day windows with identical scope and use `impact` descriptively. New pages have no same-page before baseline. Record editorial and distribution changes separately. This research run creates no deployment record and cannot show traffic impact.

## Operating model and sequence

A named editor owns evidence and corrections; a qualified practitioner reviews consequential interpretations. AI can collect and normalize source statements but cannot supply practitioner authority. Neither role's actual availability or weekly capacity is known.

Planning estimate: **18–30 editorial hours** to prepare one publishable scoped reference, excluding recruitment, practitioner review, implementation and paid product access; **2–4 hours per consequential refresh** until measured. These are hypotheses, not timed results. Check critical claims at publication and on material source changes, with a proposed 30-day review deadline. If overdue, mark the answer stale and narrow any current claim. Exact source update dates may be unavailable; retain retrieval dates without inventing publication dates.

At hypothetical two hours/week, a preparation block must precede a sustainable one-reference scope; updates should be occasional, with no promised weekly edition. At eight hours/week, allocate roughly three hours maintenance, two user learning, two distribution/reviewer coordination and one measurement, then adjust from actual costs. These are capacity sensitivities, not user constraints. Do not expand merely because collection is automated.

Sequence: obtain agreement on direction → secure reviewer and task participants → compare the specimen and time upkeep → decide on a bounded release → implement measurement and product together under separate authorization → expand only when new questions and capacity justify it. No approval is needed to finish this research; product adoption remains a separate decision.

## What could make this wrong

The strongest objection is that we are maintaining a labor-intensive summary of help pages that a competent assistant or existing comparison already provides. That objection can kill the direction.

These practical small-sample rules are proposed decision thresholds, not statistical validation:

| Rank / assumption | Smallest test, access and estimated effort | Continue / revise / stop |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The synthesis changes a real task outcome | Five operations leads bring a recent matching question; compare specimen with their best source, alternating order. Qualified reviewer assesses accuracy. 8–12 hours excluding recruitment. | Continue if 4/5 correctly distinguish the relevant handoff and 3/5 identify a useful conclusion beyond the substitute. Revise once for clarity; if substitutes suffice, stop the reference direction. If tasks are mainly buying, choose the buying desk instead. |
| 2. The answer is reliable enough to maintain | Time initial scope verification and two refreshes; reviewer checks critical claims and implications. Requires editor, reviewer and an agreed capacity; 6–10 hours estimated. | No unresolved critical errors and upkeep fits capacity while leaving reader/distribution time. Narrow once if needed; if still infeasible, stop publication. |
| 3. Value persists beyond one answer | Consenting follow-up after 2–4 weeks on a real new question or material revision; 3–5 hours. No manufactured news. | If at least 3/5 demonstrate another concrete use, explore selective updates. If not, keep an episodic reference only if initial usefulness justifies it; remove recurring-use claims. |
| 4. Reach is practical | After useful task results, a separately authorized bounded release and one rules-compliant distribution effort. Needs working analytics and actual channel eligibility. | Expand only with intended readers and useful outcomes. Low exposure is inconclusive. If no attainable route emerges within an agreed effort limit, change distribution before adding pages. |

Compared with the prior run, this improves **evidence coverage and competitive challenge**, not just presentation.
