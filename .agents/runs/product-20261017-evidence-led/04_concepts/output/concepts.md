# Concepts and product taste

October 17, 2026 UTC · Proposed. These are Markdown experiences, not implemented interfaces.

## A. Workflow compatibility reference — “Keep the handoff in view”

First visit: a firm operations lead searches whether Xero capture can replace an existing intake step. The page asks which output matters, then leads with a short bounded conclusion, a native/direct/external-approval route comparison and a source history. A copyable team note preserves the open dependency rather than declaring a winner. Return visit: reopen the same URL after a source change; inspect what changed in the answer, not an unrelated stream of news.

Hierarchy: question → applicability → route map → implication → source changes → team note. Editorial voice is exact and willing to say the evidence does not support equivalence. Use compact rows, source-date stamps and one clear argument. Hide neither limits nor the answer behind tabs. No hero metric about companies tracked; no maturity score; no artificial daily novelty.

The source-backed specimen, same-task substitute comparison and reusable record schema are included in the final [proposal](../../06_product_plan/output/product-decision.md#the-experience-we-want-to-create) as the canonical worked example. It compares Xero native capture, Dext-to-Xero, and the Dext/ApprovalMax route without treating an output status as proof of authorization. The exact contribution is a boundary map; history alone is already offered by the competing article. This is a desk assessment, not measured preference.

## B. Buying desk — “What would rule this tool out?”

Illustrative first visit: “We keep Xero. Is a separate capture tool worth evaluating?” A one-page purchase memo starts with disqualifying requirements, then eligible candidates, actual unresolved cost/plan questions and a meeting checklist. It links primary documents instead of invented accuracy scores. Return visit is a renewal or changed requirement, not a fabricated weekly habit.

Example content: “Needed: external approval routing. Unknown: account entitlements. Next decision: compare documented paths before asking for a quote.” This is illustrative questioning, not a recommendation to buy Dext. A feature table serves the decision; it is not the hero. Three carefully argued candidates beat 30 affiliate tiles. But BookkeeperTools already offers segmented buying guidance and a timeline; our hypothetical memo has not demonstrated a better buying answer.

## C. Practice lab — “Explain the mistake before revealing the answer”

Illustrative synthetic exercise: an invoice shows net 100, tax 20 and total 120; an AI-produced summary states total 100. Ask the learner to identify the mismatch, point to evidence and state what cannot be concluded. Reveal a worked explanation that distinguishes arithmetic from tax treatment. No real client data or claimed accounting qualification.

First visit completes one meaningful task without an account. Return visit attempts a new failure mode. Hierarchy is case → learner reasoning → evidence → explanation → next case. Taste favors short, inspectable artifacts over chat or generic praise. Compared with a prompt toolkit, the intended distinction is practice and feedback; an unreviewed solution would destroy it. A qualified practitioner must design and review consequential cases. The example is a concept sketch, not tested learning efficacy.

## Tension and choice

A sacrifices breadth for a reader who already has a concrete process question. B is more immediately purchase-oriented, C more inherently recurring. A's appeal rests on making a confusing software boundary legible and maintaining that answer. We prefer a useful current reference with selective change notes to an ambitious news cadence that may have nothing consequential to say.
